## Product direction (founder, 2026-11-09; documentation only; recorded as direction, not as an approved stage or task)

**Direction.** Drew is meant to become **reusable AI automation that integrates with the business software a company already uses** (Jobber is the first integration), **residential cleaning first**. A business owner should be able to configure, without a code change: **services, intake questions, pricing, extras, service area and scheduling rules.** This is the founder's stated direction. It changes no decision, no gate, no pilot step and no Stage 1 exit criterion, and `DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md` section 0 still wins wherever the two files differ.

**What exists today (so the direction is not read as a status).** Nothing above is built as an owner-configurable product. The pilot is a single fixed script for Drew Cleaning: one service label, size and price taken from the founder's private settings (`fast-call-handler.ts`), one Jobber Developer Test Account, texts OFF. That is correct for the controlled test and is not configurability. Configurable services, intake, pricing, extras, service area and scheduling are **PLANNED, not started, and not approved as work.**

**Naming (so the words are not confused).** This file has four product stages (1 to 4, "At a glance" below). **"Step" always means the six-step pilot plan in section 1.7.** The founder's instruction to plan "before Stage 5 simulation" means **pilot step 5, the simulated end-to-end run**; there is no fifth product stage.

**Where it sits in the authoritative roadmap (reconciled, not duplicated).** The direction is the existing milestones **CL-4** (a reusable, profile-driven cleaning agent: a new company set up from one reviewed profile without code changes, and one company can never affect another, tested) and **CL-6** (verified actions across scheduling software, Jobber first) of `DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md` 0.3, both PLANNED. **Three checkpoints the founder has set (all PLANNED; none is built; each needs the founder's own approval to start, as every task does):**
1. **Planning checkpoint before pilot step 5 (the simulated end-to-end run).** After step 4 and before step 5, the founder reviews a short written plan for how the fixed pilot values become owner-configurable (which values, where they live, who approves a change, how a change is tested). It is a review of a plan, not a build; step 5 does not start until the founder has reviewed it and says so. Whether the plan changes what step 5 simulates is the founder's decision at that checkpoint.
2. **Mandatory owner-approved rules and pricing gate before any real customer pilot.** Before Drew quotes or books for a real customer, the business owner approves, in writing, the exact rules it will apply: services offered, intake questions, prices and extras, service area, and scheduling limits. Drew uses only what the owner approved, and a change takes effect only after the owner approves it again. Nothing in the pilot plan satisfies this gate in advance. **The gate adds to, and never replaces, the gates that already exist, none of which the owner's rules approval waives or satisfies:** (a) the caller's spoken consent (yes to continue) and the AI disclosure wording, which still needs the business's legal check (`PRODUCT_CONTINUITY.md` sections 5 and 6); (b) the raw-audio decision: raw caller audio reaching OpenAI is accepted for founder tests with invented details only, and real customer calls need a separate data-terms and disclosure decision first (`PRODUCT_CONTINUITY.md`, update 2026-10-27, later); (c) whether a callback number is stored (consent, retention, a short legal check; `PRODUCT_CONTINUITY.md` section 6); (d) texts stay OFF until CL-2, and CL-5 requires the privacy and legal review (AI disclosure, recording, SMS) (`DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md` 0.3). **Evidence required before this gate can be met:** a test or recorded run showing that a business rule changed **without the owner's written approval** (a price, extra, service, service-area or scheduling limit) is **rejected**: the last approved rule keeps being used, the unapproved value is never quoted or booked, and the rejection is logged with a fixed code. **Whether the existing approval digest can do this is NOT established and is not assumed:** it is recorded as covering the approved configuration and the opening block, but whether it covers prices, extras, service area and scheduling rules has not been checked. 3. **Two-business configuration acceptance test.** Configuration counts as working only if **two different businesses** (different services, intake questions, prices, extras, service area and scheduling rules) can each be set up **by configuration alone, with no code change**, and each run through the simulated flow gives that business's own correct quote, booking and confirmation text, and **nothing from one business appears in the other's**. The test must also show the negative case: an unapproved rule change for either business is rejected (the evidence named under checkpoint 2), and each business keeps its own approved rules. The exact pass criteria are fixed at checkpoint 1 and are not written here. It is proposed as the way to show CL-4's "one company can never affect another"; CL-4's acceptance text is unchanged and the founder decides. It is a simulated acceptance test (stand-ins only), not a live one.

## The gate between stages (applies to every stage)

A stage is **complete** only when **all** of these hold, each with its evidence cited by file, commit, CI run or log (never asserted):
1. Every item in the stage has evidence at the level that item names. **Tests passing is never written as working live.**
2. Every defect found on the way has a regression test, and no protected test was changed to make a repair pass without being reported first.
3. CI is green on the pushed commit that contains the work.
4. The records (this file, `DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md` 0.3, the handoff) are updated in the same change, and the status words match the evidence.
5. Protected work is unchanged, and a secret and private-data scan of what was committed is clean.
6. The stage's live acceptance has been met (for Stage 1: see the exit criteria).
7. **The founder accepts the stage in writing, after being shown the evidence.**

**No stage begins by inference.** Finishing Stage N does not approve Stage N+1: the founder's written approval names the next stage, and each task inside it still needs its own approval (an approved stage does not approve its tasks). Each approval names one action and ends when it is done.

## Stage 1: Reliable Booking — **IN PROGRESS**

**Goal.** A caller can book a residential cleaning by phone, and what Drew books is exactly what the caller said and confirmed, checked by Jobber's own read-back. It is the live test CL-1 ("one complete Drew test call with texts OFF: real Jobber booking, verified") made reliable. Maps to `CL-1`.

**Standing limits that stay in force through Stage 1.** Texts OFF unless `FAST_TEXTS` is exactly `on` (it is not). Jobber writes only to the Developer Test Account, pinned by name and id. Emails only to the two approved test recipients, only after a Jobber-verified booking, and only when the start script is given `-Emails`. No personal detail in any log. Every restart, call, commit and push is a separate founder approval.

### 1.1 Address validation — **PLANNED** (not built)
- **Today (BUILT, TESTED OFFLINE):** the street, city and a five-digit ZIP (not all zero) are captured, in pieces if need be; the caller's state is captured or asked once (`src/us-state.ts`, `src/call-state.ts`). **There is no check that the ZIP, city and state belong together;** the address is confirmed only by the caller's yes to the read-back (by design so far: "never a postal check").
- **Needed:** a validation that catches a ZIP that does not fit the city or state, and a house number or street that cannot exist, **without guessing** and without booking an address the caller did not confirm.
- **Decision the founder must make before any build:** the source of truth. Options to be researched, none chosen: an offline table from a published government dataset; or an external address service (which sends the caller's address to a third party: a privacy, cost and consent decision). **UNVERIFIED:** which source is accurate, licensed and affordable.
- **Evidence for done:** tests for each wrong-combination case; a live call where a wrong ZIP or city is caught and corrected before the read-back; no address in any log.
